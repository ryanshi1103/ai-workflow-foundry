# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

FlowFoundry is building open, human-centered AI coordination infrastructure.
We commit to a community where people can contribute regardless of experience,
identity, background, nationality, disability, appearance, or technology choice.

We value precise engineering disagreement, honest maturity labels, careful
privacy practice, and evidence that others can reproduce.

## Expected behavior

- Be respectful, specific, and constructive.
- Critique ideas, claims, interfaces, and evidence—not people.
- Welcome questions and explain project context without gatekeeping.
- Credit prior work, contributors, data sources, and external projects.
- Disclose limitations, uncertainty, conflicts of interest, and generated content
  when they materially affect a contribution.
- Protect credentials, private content, personal data, customer information, and
  incident details.
- Respect maintainers' decisions to pause unsafe, unlicensed, unverifiable, or
  excessively broad work.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, discrimination, threats, stalking, or unwanted sexual attention.
- Personal attacks, sustained disruption, trolling, or publishing private
  communications without permission.
- Doxxing, credential exposure, or sharing private datasets, transcripts, media,
  or security details in public channels.
- Misrepresenting experimental work as production-ready, fabricating tests or
  benchmarks, or using FlowFoundry to promote AGI or human-replacement claims.
- Encouraging contributors to bypass permissions, review, licenses, safety gates,
  or responsible disclosure.

## Scope

This policy applies in repository issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews,
community spaces, project events, and public interactions where someone is
representing FlowFoundry.

## Reporting and enforcement

Report conduct concerns privately to the maintainers through a verified private
channel on the repository owner's GitHub profile. Security and privacy reports
must follow [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) and GitHub private vulnerability
reporting; never include sensitive evidence in a public conduct report.

Maintainers will review reports in confidence where possible, assess context,
and take proportionate action. Actions may include clarification, a warning,
content removal, temporary participation limits, or permanent exclusion. A
maintainer named in a report must recuse themselves when another qualified
maintainer is available.

Good-faith reporting and disagreement are not retaliation grounds. Deliberately
false or abusive reports may themselves violate this policy.
